ARTICLE

Made from fermented soybeans, _natto_ is a traditional part of many people's breakfast in Japan. But this food's strong smell and sticky texture can take some getting used to — even for Japanese people!

_Natto_ is often mixed with soy sauce and mustard, then eaten over a bowl of hot rice. And it's popular — a 2024 survey found that over 60% of people in Japan who eat rice at home eat _natto_ with it.

And a new study suggests this traditional food might have some benefits for the brain — and for mental health, too.

Researchers from Kagoshima University and the food company Mizkan studied health data from over 600 people aged 65 and over in the city of Tarumizu in Kagoshima prefecture, where _natto_ consumption is high.

They found that people who ate _natto_ at least twice a week were less likely to show signs of mild cognitive impairment. This includes problems with memory or thinking that are more serious than normal aging, but not as serious as dementia.

_Natto_ eaters were also less likely to show symptoms of depression. After researchers considered things like age and overall health, regular _natto_ eaters had about a 37% lower chance of both mild cognitive impairment and symptoms of depression.

The researchers aren't sure why _natto_ might be linked to better brain health and mental health. But _natto_ is rich in nutrients such as vitamin K, which previous studies have linked to brain health, and its effects on gut bacteria may also play a role.

However, the study does not prove that _natto_ was the reason for the difference, so more research is needed to see whether eating it can actually help prevent these problems.

Still, for people who already enjoy _natto_, the results may be one more reason to keep eating it!
