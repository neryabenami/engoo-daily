Can the connections we make online be as meaningful as those we make in the real world? The answer might depend on how old you are!

Earlier this year, Pew Research Center surveyed over 9,700 Americans aged 18 or over about their experiences with online communities. These communities could be on social media, discussion forums, video sites like YouTube, in online video games, or on hobby apps for things like reading, running or watching movies.

The survey found that 36% of US adults are in an online community — and that rises to 48% for people aged between 18 and 29.

So how do people feel about the connections they make there?

Well, 18% of those in online communities said they feel very close to other group members. Another 37% said they feel somewhat close. And 45% said they don't feel very close to other group members — but that's still more than half saying they feel at least a little close.

However, when asked if connections made online can be as meaningful as those in the real world, 47% said "no." Thirty-four percent said they can be as meaningful, and 20% said they aren't sure.

But that changes for younger people, with 54% of those aged 18 to 29 saying online relationships can be just as meaningful as those in the real world. Just 14% of those 65 and older said the same.

Most people don't go to online communities to meet new people, however. Pew found that the top three reasons people go to their favorite community are to stay informed, for fun, or to get advice or recommendations.

Meeting new people is actually second from the bottom — though there is again a big gap between younger and older users, with 58% of young users naming it as a reason.
