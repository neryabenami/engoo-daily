A lot of work goes into making a video game — the biggest games can take thousands of developers five years or more to create. But today, AI is helping with some of that work — and it may be more common than you think.

A new survey in Japan found that 86% of game developers are using generative AI as part of their work.

The survey was done by the Computer Entertainment Supplier’s Association {{prn:(CESA)|cesa}}. The results were released in a preview of the _CESA Games Industry Report 2026_, which looks at the Japanese video game industry and how it is changing.

CESA also asked its member companies what they hope AI can do for them. Many said they see it as a way to save time and help people get more work done. They also hope it can help them cut costs and make games more quickly.

But that doesn't mean AI is doing the work alone.

Many companies still have people check and correct content made by AI. Most also limit which AI tools developers can use.

CESA's Tsutomu Masuda said companies should make good use of AI while still keeping people at the center of how games are made. However, he also said that as AI becomes more widely used, companies need to think about things like copyright.

If generative AI uses copyrighted materials to learn how to do something, that can create legal problems. And in some places — including the US — content made completely by AI cannot be copyrighted at all.

The full _CESA Games Industry Report 2026_ will come out in early December. For now, the survey gives us a picture of an industry where AI and people are working side-by-side.
