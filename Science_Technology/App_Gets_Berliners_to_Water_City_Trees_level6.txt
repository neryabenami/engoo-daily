ARTICLE

Increasingly hot summers can leave city trees less green and healthy than they could be — but an app in Berlin is offering a solution.

Between 2018 and 2019, over 7,000 trees had to be cut down in Germany's capital city. Not all were cut down because of drought damage, but CityLab Berlin, a group that works to create digital services that improve people's lives in the city, said Berlin's authorities could not keep up with all the trees they had to water.

So in 2020, CityLab started a project called _Giess den Kiez_, which means "water your neighborhood." And it made an app with a map that shows over 90,000 trees across Berlin and information about them.

This app takes data from a few different places, including an official list of trees kept by the city. It also uses data from open source maps that show things like water pumps for people to use, as well as Germany's national weather service, so it can show how much rain has fallen.

Users can choose a tree on the map to look after, and record when they have watered it. They can even let everyone know they're going to take special responsibility for a tree by "adopting" it and listing it on their profile on the app.

The app also has a public channel on Slack — an online work and project management platform — where users can share ideas and make plans with others to water the city's trees more efficiently.

There are now 12,000 people signed up to _Giess den Kiez_. Anna Mehrländer from CityLab told _The Guardian_, "I was really surprised at just how deeply people care about trees — for many people it's almost like caring for a pet."
