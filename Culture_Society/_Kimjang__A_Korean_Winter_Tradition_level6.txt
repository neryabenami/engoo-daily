ARTICLE

When the weather gets colder in South Korea, some kitchens start filling up with cabbage — lots and lots of cabbage!

That's because the end of fall is the time for _kimjang_, a tradition where families and communities make and share kimchi together to prepare for winter.

The tradition is believed to have begun during Korea's Joseon Dynasty, which started in 1392 and ended in 1910. It was difficult to grow food during winter, and people didn't have refrigerators to keep food fresh.

So, making kimchi was one way to keep vegetables for the cold months.

Over time, _kimjang_ became a community tradition, and people in villages made kimchi together.

Making kimchi takes a lot of work. For traditional cabbage kimchi, people first put salt on the cabbage to remove some of its water. Later, they rinse the cabbage and add a mixture that can include garlic, ginger, Korean chili powder and fish sauce. The kimchi is then put into pots and left to ferment.

However, not everyone makes kimchi in the same way. Different areas use different ingredients — those close to the ocean may add seafood, for example. Families may also add their own ingredients, or have their own special ways to make kimchi.

In the past, people also had different ways to keep their kimchi cool. Pots of kimchi could be kept underground or even hung in wells. These days, of course, refrigerators make storing it much easier!

After a long day of making kimchi, there's traditionally something tasty waiting — boiled pork, or _suyuk_. This is eaten while drinking {{prn:_makgeolli_ ("MAK-gol-li")|_makgeolli_}}, a Korean rice wine.

And of course, after making enough kimchi for winter, it's also time to enjoy some kimchi with this meal!
