Japan has made it much more expensive for foreigners to apply for permanent residency, as the government introduces stricter rules for people who want to live in the country long term.

From October 1, the permanent residency application fee rose from 10,000 yen — about $65 — to 200,000 yen, or around {{prn:$1,300|one thousand and three hundred dollars}}, making it 20 times higher. Applicants also face new rules involving income, pension payments and Japanese language ability.

The changes come as Japan's foreign population reaches record levels. According to government data, about 3.8 million foreign residents were living in Japan in 2025, nearly 40% more than five years earlier.

People applying for permanent residency must now also have a stable income at or above the Japanese average. The latest data shows that average household income in Japan was about 5.8 million yen — a little less than {{prn:$37,000|thirty seven thousand dollars}} — in 2024.

From April 2027, applicants will also need to show that they have paid into Japan's pension system, and will receive enough in pension benefits to support them after they retire. For the first time, officials will also look at applicants' Japanese language ability and their understanding of Japanese rules and customs.

Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi says the changes are intended to address worries about the growing foreign population and to make sure that "both Japanese and foreign residents can live safely and securely."

Critics, however, say the new rules go too far.

"Many foreign nationals who have chosen to live in Japan and want to spend the rest of their lives here will be robbed of that hope," _The Mainichi_ wrote in a September editorial about the new rules.

There are also worries about the possible effect on Japan's workforce. The country's population fell by more than 3 million between 2020 and 2025, while worker shortages have become more serious as the population gets older and fewer people are of working age.
