Police in Japan have recorded nearly 16,000 bicycle traffic violations since April, according to data from the country's National Police Agency (NPA).

On April 1 of this year, the NPA started using a new "blue ticket" system, which lets police quickly fine cyclists for breaking traffic rules.

In the system's first six months, using a smartphone while riding was the most common violation, making up 43% of all tickets. Those caught riding while using a phone receive a fine of {{prn:12,000|twelve thousand}} yen, or about {{prn:$76|seventy six dollars}}.

The second most common violation was failing to stop when required, followed by riding through a red light. Other common violations included entering a railroad crossing when the gate was down, and riding on the wrong side of the road.

These violations all carry fines of between {{prn:3,000|three thousand}} yen and {{prn:7,000|seven thousand}} yen — or between about {{prn:$19|nineteen dollars}} and {{prn:$44|forty four dollars}}.

The rules must be followed by all cyclists aged 16 and over.

Tokyo had the highest number of tickets, with nearly {{prn:3,000,|three thousand}} followed by Osaka with close to {{prn:2,900|two thousand and nine hundred}}. Saitama recorded almost {{prn:1,900|one thousand and nine hundred}} violations, while Aichi had over {{prn:1,700|one thousand and seven hundred}}.

The NPA also said that bicycle accidents decreased by about 9% between April and September compared with the same time last year. However, it said it's not clear if this is because of the new ticket system.
