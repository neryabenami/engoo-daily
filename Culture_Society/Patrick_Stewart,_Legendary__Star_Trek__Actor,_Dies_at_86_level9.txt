Patrick Stewart, the British actor best known for playing Captain Jean-Luc Picard in _Star Trek_ and Professor Charles Xavier in the _X-Men_ films, has died at 86.

Stewart died on October 28 in Los Angeles, following a brief battle with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia.

"We lost one of the greats of stage and screen today," his _X-Men_ co-star Hugh Jackman wrote on social media. "And, in my book, a tremendous friend and man."

Although Stewart became famous for his screen roles, he spent much of his career onstage.

Born in Mirfield, Yorkshire, in northern England, Stewart grew up in a working-class family, and later spoke openly about witnessing domestic violence as a child. He joined an amateur drama group at 12 and developed a love for Shakespeare.

"The stage would prove to be a safe space, a refuge from real life in which I could inhabit another person, living in another place and time," he later wrote.

He attended the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School and spent 14 years with the Royal Shakespeare Company, performing in numerous Shakespeare plays.

Stewart's television breakthrough came in 1987 with _Star Trek: The Next Generation._ He initially expected the series to fail, but instead, it ran for seven seasons and 178 episodes, followed by four feature films.

His Shakespearean training helped him portray the starship commander, whose formal manner reminded him of characters he had played onstage.

In 2000, Stewart appeared as Professor Charles Xavier in the first _X-Men_ film. He continued playing the character in several more movies, including 2017's _Logan_ and 2022's _Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness._

Throughout his career, Stewart continued working in theater, earning three Olivier Awards and a Tony Award nomination for _Macbeth_. He also won a Grammy in 1996 for his spoken-word recording of _Peter and the Wolf._

He was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II in 2010.

Stewart also became popular with younger audiences through his humor and social media posts. He appeared in _The Simpsons_, _Futurama_ and _The Emoji Movie_.

Stewart is survived by his wife, singer and songwriter Sunny Ozell, whom he married in 2013, and his two children.
