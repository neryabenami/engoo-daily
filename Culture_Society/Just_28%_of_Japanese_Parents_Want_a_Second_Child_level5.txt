Japan's population has been getting smaller and smaller for years.

The government wants people to have more children — but while some are happy to have one child, it might be more difficult to convince them to have a second!

Meiji Yasuda Life Insurance did a survey with {{prn:1,100|one thousand and one hundred}} parents who have one child aged between 0 and 6.

And just 28% said they would like to have another child — down from 57% in 2020.

Prices have been increasing in Japan and other parts of the world for some time, and this has put pressure on families that already have children.

In fact, the top reason given when parents were asked what's stopping them from having more children was worries about having enough money in the future.

The next biggest reason parents gave for not having another child was their age.

Around eight in 10 parents said they were feeling the impact of higher prices on their family spending. Parents are finding it especially hard to keep up with the costs of food and extracurricular activities.

When asked what would make them think about having a second child, many said an increase in their income or their partner's income would make them more likely to have another child.

The survey also found that the average monthly cost of raising one child is {{prn:43,356|forty three thousand three hundred and fifty six}} yen — about {{prn:$270 — in 2026|two hundred and seventy dollars in twenty twenty six}} , up 5% from last year.

In 2018, when the survey began, the average monthly cost was {{prn:39,240|thirty nine thousand two hundred and forty}} yen, or about {{prn:$245|two hundred and forty five dollars}}.
