ARTICLE
----------------------------------------
Why do some people protest while others stay home — even if they feel strongly about the same issues? An analysis of research covering six decades suggests it has a lot to do with social connections.

The analysis, published in _Science Advances_, looked at 181 survey-based studies done between 1965 and 2023 about participation in protest marches and demonstrations to see what makes people more likely to protest.

Unsurprisingly, high interest in politics was linked with protesting. People with more left-wing political views in particular were found to be more likely to protest.

However, this varied somewhat by time and location, with right-wing protests becoming more common in Eastern Europe between 2002 and 2016, while a 2021 survey found that in the US, the UK, France and Canada, strong right or left political affiliation was associated with protest.

Surveys from Asia and Latin America did not include political affiliation.

Social connections had a very strong effect too, with people who were members of things like workers' unions and volunteer groups more likely to participate in protests.

The researchers say this may be because these organizations can help connect their members with larger politically engaged social networks. They might also offer more opportunities {{prn:— and invitations — to|and invitations to}} take part in protests.

Social media use has also become a consistent predictor of getting involved, with regular use associated with more involvement in protests.

Traditional media like TV and newspapers weren't found to be nearly as strong a predictor for protesting. The researchers suggest this may be because even if traditional media can share basic information on protests, social media offers the social connections that push people to actually take part.

The research also found that younger people with higher levels of education were more likely to protest, as were people living in urban areas. However, the researchers say this may just be because these groups have more access to demonstrations, and time to take part.
