Hollywood loves a big-budget production. Its latest one cost {{prn:$81 billion|eighty one billion dollars}} — and the action happened in a boardroom!

On October 6, Paramount completed its takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery, bringing two of America's oldest movie studios together under the name Skydance. Including Warner's existing debt, the deal is worth nearly {{prn:$111 billion|one hundred and eleven billion dollars}}. It further concentrates power in an industry already dominated by a few companies.

The new company brings together streaming platforms HBO Max and Paramount+, TV networks CNN and CBS, and films such as _The Godfather_ and _Sinners_. It is led by billionaire David Ellison.

"Today is a historic day, not just for Skydance but for our entire industry," Ellison said.

His company bought Paramount for $8 billion last year before pursuing Warner. However, Netflix initially reached a deal to buy Warner's movie studio and streaming business in December.

Paramount continued bidding, eventually offering $31 per share for the whole company. Netflix withdrew, and Paramount and Warner agreed to merge in February.

The deal has divided Hollywood. Thousands of actors, writers and directors signed an open letter opposing it, warning of job losses and less choice for audiences. Others, including director James Cameron, supported Ellison.

There are also concerns about CNN's independence. Ellison's family has longstanding ties to President Donald Trump, who has repeatedly attacked the network's reporting.

Ellison says CNN will remain editorially independent, and its editor-in-chief, Mark Thompson, is staying.

Regulators approved the takeover, but 12 US states sued to stop it, arguing that it would reduce competition and give consumers fewer choices. The Writers Guild of America also filed a lawsuit.

Settlements reached in September cleared the way for the deal. Skydance promised to increase US film production over the next five years, put millions of dollars into a fund for workers displaced by the merger, and create a board to protect editorial independence at CNN and CBS.

But critics say these promises do not go far enough to protect jobs, preserve audience choice or safeguard independent news reporting.
