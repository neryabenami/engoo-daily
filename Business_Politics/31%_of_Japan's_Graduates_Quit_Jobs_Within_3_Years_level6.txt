In Japan, it was once common for people to work at the same company for their whole career.

This tradition, known as _shushin koyo_, or "lifetime employment," became popular after World War II. Companies offered workers secure jobs, and workers often stayed until they retired.

But lifetime employment is no longer as common as it once was, and these days, many young Japanese workers leave their jobs after just a few years.

According to a new government survey, 31% of people who graduated from university in March 2023 left their jobs within three years.

And in some industries, the number was much higher. For example, 50% of university graduates working in the accommodation and food services sector left within three years. And in education, it was 42%.

Company size also made a big difference. At large companies with at least 1,000 workers, about one in four young employees left within three years. But at companies with fewer than five workers, almost 60% of employees left.

And it's not just university graduates — 37% of high school graduates also left their jobs within three years.

However, the survey also found that fewer young people are leaving their jobs than before. In fact, this is the second year the number has fallen.

One reason may be that companies are trying harder to keep their employees. As Japan's population gets older, there are fewer workers available, so some companies are offering higher pay and better working conditions to encourage people to stay.

Japan's tradition of lifetime employment may no longer be as common as it once was, but companies are still finding ways to encourage young workers to stay longer.
