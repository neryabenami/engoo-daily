November 11 is Pocky Day in Japan. People celebrate by eating and sharing Pocky, a popular stick-shaped chocolate snack.

But why November 11?

Well, November 11 can be written as 11-11, which looks like four Pocky sticks!

And Pocky isn't alone on that day. The Japan Anniversary Association has 66 commemorative days registered for November 11, including Noodle Day and Fish Sausage Day. Many of these days celebrate long, thin foods that look like the number 1.

So, where do all these special days come from?

Well, companies and industry groups can create them to promote their products or raise awareness of something. The Japan Anniversary Association then registers them.

And in recent years, the association has registered more than 200 new special days each year!

Many dates are chosen using clever number wordplay.

For example, Nori-Japan, a group that promotes the health benefits of seaweed, created "Black Seaweed Day" on September 6. In Japanese, nine and six can be read as {{prn:_ku_|koo}} and _ro._ Together, they make _kuro_, the Japanese word for "black."

For the first Black Seaweed Day, Nori-Japan gave out free seaweed sheets at Tokyo Tower, and food trucks served dishes made with seaweed.

These special days aren't only about food, though.

For example, November 26 is "Good Bath Day." In Japanese, {{prn:1-1|one one}} can be read as {{prn:_ii_|e}}, and {{prn:2-6|two six}} can be read as _fu-ro_ — and  _{{prn:ii|e}} furo_ means "good bath"!

The Japan Anniversary Association says it has registered more than 3,000 special commemorative days since it began in 1991.

The association has also come up with some fun ideas for "commemorative years." For example, 2028 could become "Garden Year" because the numbers 2 and 8 together can be read as _niwa_ — or "garden" in Japanese.

So if special days aren't enough, perhaps special years could be next!
