Japan is making it harder for foreigners to run businesses in the country — and many business owners are leaving as a result.

In the first six months of 2026, {{prn:953|nine hundred and fifty three}}  foreign residents with business manager visas left Japan, according to the country's Immigration Services Agency (ISA).

That's about four times as many as left during the first half of 2025.

According to the ISA, these people effectively left the country permanently, since they did not complete the procedures to reenter Japan on this visa.

The number of business owners leaving the country has been increasing since October 2025, when Japan introduced stricter requirements for the business manager visa.

Under the requirements, business owners need to have at least 30 million yen — about {{prn:$190,000|one hundred and ninety thousand dollars}} — in capital. That's up from 5 million yen, or about {{prn:$32,000|thirty two thousand dollars}}.

They also need to have at least one full-time worker who is a citizen or permanent resident of Japan.

Those who had a business visa before October 2025 have until October 2028 to meet the requirements.

However, Fumio Harima, an immigration specialist in Niigata City, told _The Japan Times_ that many visa holders are finding it difficult to meet the capital requirement.

Between January and September 2025, around 40 business visa holders left Japan each month without reentry permission. That increased to 80 in November that year, and {{prn:114|one hundred and fourteen}} in December.

In June 2026, {{prn:226|two hundred and twenty six}} business visa holders left.

Before the new requirements were introduced, Japan's business manager visa had become very popular. At the end of 2025, {{prn:46,781|forty six thousand seven hundred and eighty one}} people held the visa — nearly {{prn:20,000|twenty thousand}} more than five years earlier.

The government said the changes were needed because some people may have been using the visa mainly as a way to live in Japan — with some cases involving businesses that were not actually operating.
