You arrive at a popular restaurant, hungry and ready to eat — only to find a long line outside.

Would you pay to skip the line?

More restaurants in Japan are offering exactly that. Much like fast passes at theme parks, restaurant "fast-pass" services let diners pay an extra fee for priority entry.

One service, called {{prn:SuiSui|すいすい}}, is now available at more than 100 restaurants across 20 of Japan's 47 prefectures. Popular among younger people and tourists looking to save time, it's mostly used at busy restaurants in city centers and tourist areas, where long lines are common.

Using the service is simple. Customers scan a QR code outside a busy restaurant and buy a fast-pass ticket on their phone. Tickets usually cost between 500 yen and 2,000 yen — about {{prn:$3|three dollars}} to {{prn:$13|thirteen dollars}} — and prices can change depending on how busy the restaurant is. At some very popular restaurants, they can cost as much as 5,000 yen, or about {{prn:$32|thirty two dollars}}.

{{prn:SuiSui|すいすい}} began testing the idea in early 2023, and officially launched the service in October that year. The company says around 50,000 people have now used it. Restaurants offering {{prn:SuiSui|すいすい}} include well-known names such as Yabaton, famous for its miso katsu, and ramen chain Tsujita.

And there seems to be demand for shorter waits. A 2024 Gurunavi survey of over {{prn:1,200|one thousand and two hundred}} people found that 30 minutes was the most common maximum wait time people would accept to enter a restaurant. Only about 15% would wait an hour or more.

But not everyone is happy about the service. Some feel it's unfair that customers with more money can jump ahead of others.

Others don't see the point. As one Reddit user wrote: "There are so many good places to eat in Japan. I have no idea why someone would bother waiting in line."
