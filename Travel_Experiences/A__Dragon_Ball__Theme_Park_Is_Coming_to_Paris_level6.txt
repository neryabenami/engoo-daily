For years, fans have followed Goku and his friends through the pages of _Dragon Ball_ manga, and through the popular TV series and movies of the same name.

And soon, they could step into that world themselves, with a new theme park set to be built in France.

The park will be built near Paris, as part of a $7 billion deal with Saudi Arabia.

The idea came about after French President Emmanuel Macron and Saudi Arabia's Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman realized in a 2025 meeting that they had a shared love for _Dragon Ball_.

A year later, the project has been given the green light, with Saudi Arabia confirming it will fund three new theme parks in France. Just one of the parks will be dedicated to _Dragon Ball_, though.

The parks will be funded through Saudi Arabia's sports and entertainment-focused Qiddiya Investment Company, part of the country's efforts to find ways to make money from things other than oil.

In 2024, Saudi Arabia already announced that it would build the world's first _Dragon Ball_ theme park in Qiddiya City, near Riyadh. That park is still under construction.

As for the parks in France, according to French media, they could be built on the site of the old Mirapolis park in Val-d'Oise, which closed in 1991. Val-d'Oise is just north of Paris.

"Nothing like this has been seen since Disneyland Paris," Macron said in an online post. He added that the deal is expected to create 22,000 new jobs.

No opening date has been announced, and it's thought the parks will take several years to construct.

_Dragon Ball_ first came out as a manga series in 1984, and the first anime series was released in 1986. Since then, it's become a huge global franchise, with movies, video games and more.
