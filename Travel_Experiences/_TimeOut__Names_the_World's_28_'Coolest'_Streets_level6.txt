What makes a part of a city "cool"? Is it clubs? Unique local shops?

_TimeOut_ has released its list of the world's 28 "coolest" streets for 2026. And according to the magazine, a "cool" street is a place where people "want to spend their time right now." That means these streets are "home to some of the very best places to eat, drink, dance, shop and hang out" in each city.

The magazine asked its editors and local experts for their ideas, and they decided that the coolest street in the world for 2026 is Song Wat Road in Bangkok, Thailand.

This {{prn:1,200-meter-long|one thousand and two hundred meter long}} street was once an important trade hub between Bangkok's Chinatown and the Chao Phraya River. But these days, its old brick warehouses are full of restaurants, colorful street art, independent fashion stores and more!

In second place is London's Golborne Road. It's known for its mix of Portuguese, Moroccan and Middle Eastern restaurants, as well as bars, cafes and vintage clothing shops.

In third place is Fernández de Enciso in Buenos Aires, Argentina. This street has lots of cafes and restaurants where visitors can sit outside and eat under the trees. Nearby Plaza Arenales is also a popular meeting place, where there are often events and live music.

Four of _TimeOut'_s 10 coolest streets are in Asia, with Singapore's Neil Road at number five, Hong Kong's Upper Lascar Row at number seven, and Beach Street in George Town, Malaysia, at number nine.

There are also two Japanese streets on the list.

Sanjo-dori in Kyoto is in 13th place. It's known for its old red-brick buildings, many of which are now cafes, galleries and shops. Oyafuko Street in Fukuoka is number 25.

_TimeOut_ said its list shows the "variety of street life" around the world.
