For many years, they looked over the seas and helped guide ships to safety and welcome them home.

Now, some lighthouses in Japan are welcoming tourists instead!

There are about {{prn:3,100 | three thousand and one hundred}} lighthouses in Japan, according to _Kyodo News_. However, some of these lighthouses are no longer in use.

In 2021, the Japan Coast Guard came up with a plan to look after these lighthouses and turn them into places tourists and residents could enjoy.

Under the plan, local groups help take care of the lighthouses, and also organize tours and events to bring in visitors.

So far, 62 organizations across Japan have joined the initiative, looking after 68 lighthouses.

For example, an organization in Toyama prefecture opens Ikujihana Lighthouse to visitors once a month between June and October. People can enjoy beautiful views of the sea and nearby mountains from the lighthouse.

Another lighthouse, in Aichi prefecture, has become a popular place for school trips. Children can learn about its history, walk around inside and even try turning on its light!

"We want people to learn about the history of lighthouses and the role they've long played in keeping ships safe," a member of the coast guard told _Kyodo News_.

Other countries are also finding ways to give old lighthouses a new life. Visitors to the Harlingen Lighthouse in the Netherlands, for example, can actually sleep there!
